Solana Ghana Community

Building a Ghana-first on-ramp for Solana builders

August 31, 2026 • 3 min read

Ghana has no shortage of people who can build. What it has a shortage of is a clear route from “I want to build on-chain” to a shipped, working thing — with someone to ask when you get stuck at step three.

Solana Ghana is my attempt at that route.

The gap

Most people I meet in Accra hit the same three walls. The first is orientation: the ecosystem moves fast enough that last year’s tutorial is this year’s dead end, and it is genuinely hard to tell which is which from the outside. The second is money — devnet is free, but the mental model of accounts, rent, and program-derived addresses costs time that people with jobs do not have. The third is company: building alone is slow, and there is no one to review the thing you just wrote at 2 AM.

None of these are solved by another blog post. They are solved by structure.

What the community actually is

Three things, deliberately small:

Learning tracks. A curated, ordered path rather than a pile of links — accounts and rent first, then instructions and PDAs, then CPIs, then testing. Each step ends in something that runs, not something you have read.

Builder routes. Once the basics land, a route into real work: an open-source issue, a small protocol integration, or a project someone in the group is already shipping. The point is to make the second project easier to start than the first.

Events. In-person where possible. Most of the unblocking that matters happens in the ten minutes after a session ends.

Why I care

I build payment infrastructure — ChainPay settles on Solana, and much of what I have learned about accounts, token programs, and settlement came from doing it badly first. That knowledge is cheap to pass on and expensive to rediscover. A Ghana-first on-ramp is the highest-leverage thing I can do with it.

The longer bet is simpler: infrastructure gets built by people who live with the problem. Ghana has plenty of payment problems worth solving. It should also have the people who can solve them on-chain.

Getting involved

Come find the community at solana-ghana-community.onrender.com, or reach me on twitter. If you are already building on Solana in Ghana and I have not met you yet, that is the gap I most want to close.
